# Kora campus pilot kit

Status: prepared, not recruited or run. The numbers below are proposed study design choices, not observed results or market benchmarks.

## Recruitment and stages

Start with five moderated usability sessions, then a four-week pilot with 50–100 consenting freshers across two or three institutions. Include modest Android phones, participants who use assistive technology, students with intermittent connectivity and students who prefer Pidgin. Recruit through authorised course representatives or academic-support teams. No outreach has been sent by the app or agent.

Use study IDs in research files. Keep any separately consented contact list outside the repository and restrict it to the human research team. Invite people who stop using the app to an optional exit interview. Do not make participation, feedback or sharing activity a condition of academic support or access to Kora.

## Participant information script

“We are testing whether Kora makes semester planning and short study sessions useful for freshers. Joining is optional. It does not affect your grades, university standing or access to the app. You can skip questions or stop. The app keeps learning data on your device. We will ask you separately before observing your screen, collecting a study summary, recording an interview or contacting you again. A summary contains counts and relative-week activity, not your name, grades or exact study dates. A full backup contains much more personal information and is not requested for this study. We cannot promise improved grades.”

Before consent, the research lead must supply their real name/contact, the study's actual purpose, approved retention period, withdrawal process and any institution-required research approval. Do not recruit until these details are complete. Use separate choices for participation, screen observation, summary sharing, recording and recontact. Set a deletion date; do not retain data indefinitely. Summaries already aggregated without identifiers may not be removable by participant after de-identification; explain the actual process before collecting them.

## Moderated tasks

1. Ask the student what academic task they currently manage with another tool. Observe how they interpret Kora's first screen and storage choices.
2. Ask them to add two real or invented courses, identify the difference between a recorded grade and a scenario, and explain what a target estimate means. They may use invented grades throughout.
3. Add one real or invented deadline and a realistic seven-day time budget. Have them adjust the suggested plan. Ask whether the chosen next step is useful and why.
4. Complete one study action, answer a practice question, revisit an error and explain what they expect to happen next.
5. Disconnect, relaunch, export a test backup and restore it. Ask which data would be lost if browser storage were cleared.
6. Show the voluntary activity summary preview. Ask which information they believe would be shared; only collect it with separate consent.

Record independent completion, help required, wording misunderstood and severity. Never correct a task silently and then score it as independent success.

## Measures and decisions

| Question                                           | Method                                                       | Interpretation                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Can a fresher create a useful plan in one sitting? | Observation plus participant explanation of its usefulness   | Proposed usability target: at least four of the first five complete core planning independently. If not, fix the recurring problem before expanding recruitment.                                                             |
| Do learners return in weeks two and four?          | Consented follow-up; optionally the voluntary study summary  | The summary observes recorded actions from the first study action, not installation or app opens. Week two covers relative days 7–13; week four covers 21–27. Only include complete observation windows in each denominator. |
| Does the plan survive real changes?                | Interviews plus self-reported rescheduling examples          | Ask why the learner changed or abandoned sessions. Current plan state does not establish historical activation or causality.                                                                                                 |
| Does offline use work on actual phones?            | Device protocol and incidents                                | Report device/browser/version, task, outcome and recovery. Count unfinished updates separately from data loss.                                                                                                               |
| What creates continuing value?                     | Weekly short interviews and exit interviews                  | Compare planning, practice and recovery value; include non-returning students. Do not equate streaks or action counts with learning.                                                                                         |
| Is there a sustainable buyer?                      | Separate interviews with students and academic-support staff | Ask about existing spending, procurement and willingness to sponsor support. Do not infer willingness to pay from positive feedback.                                                                                         |

Do not invent retention targets from this small exploratory study. Predeclare the observation windows and report participant counts, complete-window denominators, missing data and reasons for dropout. Assess grade outcomes only through a separately designed, consented study.

## Sharing data

Use **Your data → Share a study activity summary → Preview the exact summary**. Participants decide whether to download and share it. There is no server collection. The summary omits identity, grades, course titles, answers, precise dates and raw sessions. It includes observation length, action counts, distinct active days, completed planned sessions, reviewed-question count and relative-week activity. Small summaries can still reveal habits in context; restrict researcher access and follow the agreed retention period.

New demo personas and simulated clocks are excluded. Older imported demos may lack a marker; ask participants whether they used demo history and exclude it manually. Restored/missing history and self-reported completion limit interpretation. Never request a full learner backup as the default research submission.
